How to study for a test (tips that actually work)

Reading your notes over and over feels like studying, but it's one of the least effective ways. Here's what science says actually works.

1. Quiz yourself

Close your notes and try to remember. Flashcards, practice problems and practice tests all count. Pulling information out of your brain makes it stick much better than putting it in again.

2. Spread it out

Studying 20 minutes a day for 5 days beats 2 hours the night before. Start a few days early.

3. Explain it out loud

Teach it to a friend, a parent or even your pet. If you can't explain it simply, that's the part to study more.

4. Focus on what's hard

It's tempting to practice what you already know. Spend most of your time on the topics that confuse you.

5. Mix it up

Mixing different kinds of problems (instead of 20 of the same) helps you learn when to use each method, just like on the real test.

6. Take breaks

Try 25 minutes of focus, then a 5-minute break. Put your phone in another room while you study.

7. Sleep

Your brain stores what you learned while you sleep. An all-nighter usually makes test scores worse, not better.

8. On test day

Eat breakfast, read every question carefully, and do the easy questions first to build confidence.

Common mistakes

  • Highlighting everything and calling it studying.
  • Cramming everything the night before.
  • Only studying what you're already good at.
