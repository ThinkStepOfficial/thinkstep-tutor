Area and circumference of a circle

Circles have their own special number: pi (π), about 3.14. With it, you can measure any circle.

Words to know

  • Radius (r): from the center to the edge.
  • Diameter (d): all the way across through the center. d = 2 × r.
  • Circumference (C): the distance around the circle.

Circumference

C = π × d   or   C = 2 × π × r

A circle with diameter 10 cm: C = 3.14 × 10 = 31.4 cm.

Area

A = π × r²

A circle with radius 5 cm: A = 3.14 × 5 × 5 = 3.14 × 25 = 78.5 cm².

Remember which is which: area is about the space inside, so it's measured in square units (cm²) and uses r². Circumference is a length, so it's just cm.

Try one yourself

A pizza has a diameter of 12 inches. What is its area? (Hint: find the radius first.)

Common mistakes

  • Using the diameter in the area formula instead of the radius.
  • Forgetting to square the radius.
  • Leaving off square units (²) on area answers.
