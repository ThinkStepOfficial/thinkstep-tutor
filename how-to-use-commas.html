How to use commas correctly

A comma tells the reader to pause briefly. Here are the five comma rules you'll use most.

1. In a list

Put commas between three or more things: I packed apples, crackers, and juice. (The comma before "and" is optional, but many teachers like it.)

2. Joining two full sentences

Use a comma before and, but, or, so, yet when they join two complete sentences: It started raining, so we went inside.

3. After an introduction

Put a comma after an opening word or phrase: After lunch, we played soccer. However, it was cold.

4. Around extra information

If you could remove a part and the sentence still works, put commas around it: My brother, who is ten, loves robots.

5. Dates, addresses and names

July 4, 1776 · Paris, France · Thanks, Sam! (when you speak to someone directly).

Comma splice alert: a comma alone can't join two full sentences. "I was tired, I went to bed" is wrong. Fix it: "I was tired, so I went to bed" or "I was tired. I went to bed."

Try one yourself

Add commas: Yes I finished my math science and reading but I still need to practice piano.

Common mistakes

  • Comma splices (joining two sentences with just a comma).
  • Putting a comma between a subject and its verb: My dog, barked. ✗
  • Forgetting the second comma around extra information.
