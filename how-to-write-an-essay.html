How to write an essay (the 5-paragraph way)

A great essay is like a hamburger: a top bun (introduction), the fillings (body paragraphs), and a bottom bun (conclusion). Here's how to build one.

Before you write: plan

Pick your main idea and three reasons that support it. Jot them down as a quick outline. Planning first makes writing much faster.

Paragraph 1: introduction

  • Start with a hook that grabs attention: a question, a surprising fact or a short story.
  • Give a little background.
  • End with your thesis statement: one sentence that says your main point and your three reasons.

Paragraphs 2–4: body

Each body paragraph covers one reason:

  1. A topic sentence that names the reason.
  2. Evidence: an example, fact or quote.
  3. Explanation: how the evidence supports your point.
  4. A closing sentence that links back to your thesis.

Paragraph 5: conclusion

Restate your thesis in new words, sum up your three reasons, and end with a final thought, like why it matters or what the reader should do.

Use transition words to connect ideas: first, also, another reason, for example, however, finally, in conclusion.

Try it yourself

Topic: "Should students have homework?" Write a thesis and three reasons.

Common mistakes

  • No clear thesis, so the reader doesn't know your point.
  • Putting two big ideas in one body paragraph.
  • Adding brand-new ideas in the conclusion.
