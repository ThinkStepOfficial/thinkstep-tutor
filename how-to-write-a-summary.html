How to write a summary

A summary tells the most important ideas of something you read, in your own words, and much shorter than the original.

The steps

  1. Read the whole thing first, without writing.
  2. Find the main idea. Ask: what is this mostly about?
  3. Pick 2–4 key details that support the main idea. Skip small details and examples.
  4. Write it in your own words. Look away from the text while you write.
  5. Keep it short and don't add your opinion.

For stories: Somebody Wanted But So Then

Somebody (main character) wanted (goal) but (problem) so (what they did) then (how it ended).

Cinderella wanted to go to the ball, but her stepmother wouldn't let her, so her fairy godmother helped her go, then the prince found her with the glass slipper.

Summary vs retelling: a retelling includes everything. A summary only keeps the big ideas.

Try one yourself

Summarize your favorite movie in just 2 sentences using Somebody Wanted But So Then.

Common mistakes

  • Copying sentences straight from the text.
  • Including too many small details.
  • Adding your own opinion ("I loved this story because...").
