Place value, explained simply

In 555, every digit is a 5, but each one is worth something different. That's place value: a digit's value depends on where it sits.

The place value chart

Thousands | Hundreds | Tens | Ones . Tenths | Hundredths
    3    |    4    |  7  |  2  .   5   |    1

3,472.51

Each place is 10 times bigger than the place to its right. Ten ones make a ten, ten tens make a hundred, and so on.

Expanded form

You can break a number into the value of each digit: 3,472 = 3,000 + 400 + 70 + 2.

Decimals

Places after the decimal point get smaller: tenths, hundredths, thousandths. In 0.51, the 5 means 5 tenths and the 1 means 1 hundredth.

Zero is a placeholder. In 405, the 0 tells you there are no tens. Without it, 405 would turn into 45!

Try one yourself

In 8,206.3, what is the value of the 2? The 6? The 3?

Common mistakes

  • Saying the digit instead of its value (the 4 in 3,472 is worth 400, not 4).
  • Calling tenths "tens".
  • Leaving out zeros in expanded form.
