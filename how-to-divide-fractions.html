How to divide fractions (keep, change, flip)

Dividing fractions has a famous three-word trick: keep, change, flip. Once you know it, every fraction division turns into a multiplication.

Keep, change, flip

  1. Keep the first fraction the same.
  2. Change the ÷ sign to ×.
  3. Flip the second fraction upside down (this is called its reciprocal).

Then multiply like normal.

3/4 ÷ 1/2 = 3/4 × 2/1 = 6/4 = 1 1/2

Does that make sense? There are one and a half halves in three quarters. Yes!

Why does flipping work?

Dividing by a number asks "how many of these fit?" How many halves fit in 3? Six, because each whole holds 2 halves. That's the same as 3 × 2. Dividing by 1/2 is the same as multiplying by 2, its flip.

Whole numbers and mixed numbers

Put whole numbers over 1, and turn mixed numbers into improper fractions first. For example, 2 1/2 ÷ 5 = 5/2 ÷ 5/1 = 5/2 × 1/5 = 5/10 = 1/2.

Try one yourself

2/3 ÷ 4/9 = ?

Hint: keep 2/3, change to ×, flip 4/9. Then simplify.

Common mistakes

  • Flipping the first fraction instead of the second.
  • Forgetting to change ÷ to ×.
  • Flipping a mixed number without making it an improper fraction first.
