Photosynthesis, explained simply

Plants can't go to the store for food, so they make their own. They use sunlight to turn water and air into sugar. That's photosynthesis.

What goes in, what comes out

carbon dioxide + water —(sunlight)→ glucose + oxygen

6CO₂ + 6H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂

  • Carbon dioxide comes from the air, through tiny holes in leaves called stomata.
  • Water comes up from the roots.
  • Sunlight gives the energy.
  • Glucose (a sugar) is the plant's food.
  • Oxygen goes out into the air, which is what we breathe!

Where it happens

Inside leaf cells are tiny parts called chloroplasts. They hold chlorophyll, a green substance that catches sunlight. That's why most leaves are green.

Memory trick: photo means light and synthesis means putting together. Photosynthesis = putting together (food) with light.

Why it matters

Almost every food chain starts with plants doing photosynthesis, and it makes the oxygen animals need. Plants and animals help each other: we breathe out carbon dioxide, plants use it.

Check yourself

What are the three things a plant needs for photosynthesis? What two things does it make?

Common mistakes

  • Thinking plants get their food from the soil. Soil gives water and minerals, but the food is made in the leaves.
  • Mixing up what goes in (CO₂) and what comes out (O₂).
  • Forgetting that sunlight is energy, not an ingredient.
