The water cycle, explained

The water you drink today might once have been in a dinosaur's puddle! Earth's water keeps moving in a loop called the water cycle.

The four main stages

  1. Evaporation: the sun heats water in oceans, lakes and rivers, and it turns into an invisible gas called water vapor that rises into the air. (Plants give off water vapor too. That's called transpiration.)
  2. Condensation: high up, the air is cooler, so the vapor cools and turns back into tiny droplets. Millions of droplets together make clouds.
  3. Precipitation: when droplets join and get too heavy, they fall as rain, snow, sleet or hail.
  4. Collection: the water gathers in oceans, lakes, rivers and underground (groundwater). Then the cycle starts again.

See it at home: breathe on a cold mirror and watch it fog up. That's condensation! A puddle drying up on a sunny day is evaporation.

Why it matters

The water cycle brings fresh water to land, fills rivers and lakes, and waters plants and crops. The same water has been cycling around for billions of years.

Check yourself

Put these in order: precipitation, evaporation, collection, condensation. What makes the water evaporate?

Common mistakes

  • Mixing up evaporation (liquid → gas) and condensation (gas → liquid).
  • Thinking clouds are made of water vapor. They're made of tiny liquid droplets or ice crystals.
  • Forgetting that the sun powers the whole cycle.
