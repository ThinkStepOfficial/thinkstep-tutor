How to simplify fractions to lowest terms

Simplifying a fraction means writing it with the smallest numbers possible, without changing how much it's worth. {F(4,8)} and {F(1,2)} are the same amount.

The method

  1. Find a number that divides both the top and bottom. The biggest one is called the greatest common factor (GCF).
  2. Divide the top and bottom by it.
  3. Check again. If they still share a factor, divide again.
12/18 → ÷6 → 2/3

12 and 18 are both in the 6 times table, so divide both by 6.

Don't know the GCF? Go step by step

You can divide by small numbers again and again. 12/18: both even, so ÷2 gives 6/9. Both in the 3 times table, so ÷3 gives 2/3. Same answer!

Quick checks: both even? Divide by 2. Both end in 0 or 5? Divide by 5. Digits of both add up to a multiple of 3? Divide by 3.

How do you know you're done?

A fraction is in lowest terms when the only number that divides both the top and bottom is 1. 2/3 is done, because nothing except 1 goes into both 2 and 3.

Common mistakes

  • Dividing only the top or only the bottom.
  • Subtracting instead of dividing (4/8 is not 2/6!).
  • Stopping too early, like leaving 6/9 instead of 2/3.
