How to round numbers

Rounding makes numbers simpler while keeping them close to the real value. There's just one rule to remember: 5 or more, round up.

The steps

  1. Find the place you're rounding to (tens, hundreds, tenths...). Underline that digit.
  2. Look at the digit just to its right.
  3. 5 or more? Add 1 to the underlined digit. 4 or less? Leave it alone.
  4. Change everything to the right into zeros (or drop it, after a decimal point).

Examples

Round 347 to the nearest ten: the tens digit is 4, the next digit is 7 (5 or more), so → 350.

Round 347 to the nearest hundred: the hundreds digit is 3, the next digit is 4 (4 or less), so → 300.

Round 6.283 to the nearest tenth: the tenths digit is 2, the next digit is 8, so → 6.3.

Think of a number line: 347 sits between 340 and 350. It's closer to 350, so that's where it rounds.

What about 9s?

Round 396 to the nearest ten: the 9 goes up by one and becomes 10, so it carries over. 396 → 400.

Try one yourself

Round 2,651 to the nearest hundred, and 4.75 to the nearest whole number.

Common mistakes

  • Looking at the wrong digit. Always look one place to the right.
  • Rounding down when the digit is exactly 5. 5 rounds up.
  • Forgetting to turn the digits after it into zeros.
